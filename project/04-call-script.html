The sales conversation framework

A framework, not
a script to read aloud.

Learn the structure. Internalize the questions. Use their exact words back at them. The close writes itself.

Phase 01

The Opening

First 2 min
You say →
"Thanks for jumping on, [Name]. Before I show you anything, I just want to understand your situation a bit better. Is that okay?"
Why this works: Asking permission sets a consultative tone immediately. They expect a pitch — you give them a conversation. The word "okay?" invites them in rather than pushing.
Phase 02

Discovery Questions

5–7 min
Q1
"Where are most of your leads coming from right now — ads, referrals, Instagram?"
Sets the context. Reveals which channels they trust and invest in.
Q2
"When someone sends you a WhatsApp message or fills a form, how quickly do you typically get back to them?"
This is the money question. Whatever they say, it's too slow. Let them feel that.
Q3
"What happens to leads that come in after hours or on weekends?"
Most say "we reply Monday." That's your gap. Don't say it yet — just note it.
Q4
"Do you have any follow-up system in place, or is it mostly manual?"
Reveals the chaos behind the scenes. Let them describe it without judgment.
Q5
"On average, how many inquiries would you say you get per week?"
Sizing the problem. Under 10 = not the right fit. 20+ = strong ROI case.
Q6
"And roughly, what percentage of those actually convert to bookings or sales?"
They usually guess low. This becomes the before metric in your case study.
Listening rule →
"Their answers are your pitch. Use their words back to them, not yours."
Don't take notes on a device. Write on paper. It signals you're listening, not processing.
Phase 03

The Presentation

5–8 min
Bridge into the demo →
"Okay — based on what you've told me, you're getting around [X] leads per week, response time is [Y hours], and about [Z%] are converting. That's actually really common, and the good news is it's fixable."
Then: show the demo video or walk through the flow live. Let the WhatsApp conversation sell the concept before you mention price.
After the demo →
"What I build sits on top of your WhatsApp and handles everything from the first message to the booked appointment. Your team only gets involved when the lead is warm and ready."
Key phrase: "warm and ready." It repositions the system from a tool to a filter for their team's time.
Phase 04

Handling Objections

As needed
Objection

"We already have someone managing WhatsApp."

Response

"That's great — this doesn't replace them. It frees them to focus on warm leads only. The system handles all first responses and filtering."

Objection

"I'm not sure about the price."

Response

"Totally fair. How much is one new client worth to you? [Pause.] If this brings you even 3–5 extra clients a month you were losing, does the investment make sense?"

Objection

"I need to think about it."

Response

"Of course. What's the main thing you'd want to be clearer on before deciding? I'd rather answer that now than have you sitting with a question."

Objection

"Can you show me results from another client?"

Response

"Absolutely — I worked with a booking agent for a salon in Italy and a recruitment bot for a German company. Let me pull that up."

Phase 05

The Close

Final 3 min
You say →
"Based on what we've discussed, I think the [Standard/Growth] package makes the most sense for you because [specific reason from their answers]. The setup fee is [$X] and then [$Y]/month. I can have the first version live within [X] days. Does that work, or is there anything you'd want to adjust?"
Always recommend a specific plan with a reason. Never say "it depends" or "what's your budget?" — that hands control to them. The close is a recommendation, not a menu.
Rule 01

Diagnose first.

A doctor doesn't prescribe before asking symptoms. Understand before you sell.

Rule 02

Silence wins.

After the price — pause. Don't fill the space. The first person to speak loses.

Rule 03

Make it concrete.

"Three extra clients a month" beats "more revenue." Specifics feel real. Abstractions feel risky.
